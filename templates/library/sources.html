Everything in the library is free to use. The old works are in the public domain; the modern datasets are shared under Creative Commons licences, which ask us to say where they come from. Thank you to everyone who made them.

Public domain

  • Matthew Henry’s Concise Commentary (1706) and C. H. Spurgeon’s Treasury of David (1869–85), from the CrossWire Bible Society’s SWORD modules.
  • Nave’s Topical Bible (Orville J. Nave, 1896), from the CrossWire SWORD module.
  • Easton’s Bible Dictionary (M. G. Easton, 1897).
  • Hitchcock’s Bible Names Dictionary (Roswell D. Hitchcock, 1869), via BibleData by Brady Stephenson (CC BY 4.0).
  • The creeds, confessions and catechisms, from the Creeds.json project (only the texts it marks as public domain).
  • The Gospel harmony is built from the section headings of the Berean Standard Bible (public domain).
  • The map is drawn from Natural Earth (public domain), with no modern borders.

Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0)

  • People, places, events and the Easton’s text: Theographic Bible Metadata by Robert Rouse (theographic.com), github.com/robertrouse/theographic-bible-metadata. Locations draw on the OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding Data (CC BY 4.0).
  • Parallel passages and the Old Testament in the New: UBS Parallel Passage Database, © 2023 United Bible Societies.
  • Journeys: UBS Project MARBLE Bible Routes by Dr Leen Ritmeyer, © United Bible Societies 2023.

We have reshaped these datasets (for example, merging them into one file and linking references). Our adapted versions of the CC BY-SA data, in static/bibles/library.sqlite, are shared under the same licence: creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0.
